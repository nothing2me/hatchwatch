# Pole anchoring for low-budget ground teams

**Status:** planning research for the HatchWatch pole-mounted camera housing
(`docs/HOUSING.md`). Figures are planning estimates from typical coastal
hardware; field validation (pull-out tests, seasonal weather, permitting) is
required before deployment. The pole is assumed to be 40 mm (1.5″) PVC or
galvanized steel, ~2.4–3 m above the sand, housing at ~2 m.

## The problem

A single tall mast in wind behaves like a lever: the wind force on the housing
(solar panel too) multiplies at the base. Plain "dig a hole" works only when
the ground is packed; dry dune sand gives almost no grip. Unattended gear on a
nesting beach also has two extra constraints: **minimize footprint/disturbance**
(NPS/NOAA guidance for sea-turtle nesting beaches) and **be removable** for
storm season and seasonal permits.

## Options (cheapest first)

| Option | Approx. cost / camera | Hold vs gust wind | Install time | Dune disturbance | Removable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deep-bury with water method + packed collar | $5–8 | Low–med | 10–15 min | Medium (dig to 45–60 cm) | Yes (re-dig) |
| Corkscrew/auger sand anchor inside a PVC sleeve (dock-auger kits fit 1.5″ Sch-40 pipe) | $13–25 | Medium | 3–5 min/leg | Very low | Yes |
| Buried post with guy ropes to helical earth anchors | $25–50 | High | 30–45 min | Low (anchors + cable lanes) | Partly (pull anchors, backfill) |
| Sand-bag ballast base (fill-on-site pouches) | $8–20 | Low (weight only) | 5 min | Negligible | Yes (empty & go) |

Supporting practice (from coastal/sand-anchor literature):

- **Auger (corkscrew) is the most effective single anchor in sand.** Dock/sand
  auger kits (~$13–17/2-pack) are PA66+30 % GF or galvanized, screw into 1.5″
  Sch-40 PVC, install with a T-handle in minutes, and hold in sand/clay without
  corrosion. Common wisdom: drive ≥ 18″ (45 cm), and double a tall pole with a
  **sand screw anchor + guy rope** for extra hold.
- **Prepare the sand first.** Damp, compacted sand holds far better than dry
  dune sand; a pilot hole + light water ("water method") recom-pacts the collar
  around the pole. Choose a spot above the high-water line and avoid loose
  dune crests.
- **Depth beats width.** 18–24″ depth with a packed collar is the cheapest
  meaningful upgrade; rocking the pole while pushing compacts sand evenly.
- **Guyed is the strongest low-tech configuration** for a 2.5 m mast with a
  sail-like solar panel: two or three 3/16″ galvanized cables from mid-mast to
  helical earth anchors (T-wrench, ~$8–15 each) absorbs gusts that would pry a
  single spade.

## Recommended low-budget spec

**Primary (balanced):** a 1.5″ Sch-40 PVC pole into a ~$15 auger/sand-anchor
sleeve mounted at the dune line above the high-water mark, plus a sand-collar
packed around the base and a single guy rope from the housing pole to a
galvanized helical anchor. Install ≈ 20–30 min/unit, ~$20–35 total per camera,
very low footprint, fully removable.

**Absolute minimum budget:** deep-bury (60 cm) the pole with the water method
and a packed collar (~$5–8). Adequate for calm-season demos; expect to
re-settle after storms or if it loosens.

**Not recommended:** bare sandbags as the only anchor for a 2.5 m mast (wind
still moves it), and concrete pours (permanent, high disturbance, conflicts
with nesting-beach practice and permits).

## Operational notes

- Remove or lower masts for hurricane season (Port Aransas storm surge will
  claim anything solo in the open; a guyed, auger-based install is
  recoverable).
- Turtle season / beach ordinance: check City of Port Aransas and TPWD rules
  for structures and markers; keep below the beach-vehicle line, mark poles,
  and avoid the primary nesting corridor where possible. The housing is a
  monitoring aid — it must never shade or obstruct nesting crawl lanes.
- Corrosion: PVC + stainless/guy hooks; if using galvanized steel, accept
  surface rust and replace cables yearly.
- Pull-out test before trusting it: try to shift the top of the pole with ~30 kg
  side force — if it rocks, add a second anchor before leaving it unattended.
